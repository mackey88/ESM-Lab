ESM Trainer

A hostile surface combatant is operating in this sector, mixed in with friendly and commercial traffic. Your job: find it, identify it, fix its position, and report it.

STEP 1

Identify

Open the signal analyzer, measure PRI and pulse width, and match the radar in the library.

STEP 2

Locate

Drop 3+ lines of bearing from different positions, then build an AOP.

STEP 3

Correlate

Label the AOP with the platform class that carries those radars.

STEP 4

Export

Download your results file and hand it in.

How you're graded (8 points per hostile platform):
  • AOP semi-major axis < 5 NM (2 pts) and semi-minor axis < 5 NM (2 pts)
  • AOP center within 5 NM of the platform's real position (2 pts)
  • Correct platform label on the AOP (2 pts)
The panel at the top left of the map always shows your next step. Follow it if you're unsure what to do.

How to run the mission

You can reopen this any time with the Help button.

1. Identify each emitter

  1. Pick an emitter in the Detected emitters list, or click its bearing line on the map.
  2. Click Analyze signal. The frequency is filled in for you.
  3. Click Measure PRI, then click the leading edge of two neighbouring pulses.
  4. Click Measure PW. The scope zooms in on one pulse. Click its left edge, then its right edge.
  5. Pick the library row whose frequency, PRI and PW all match, and click Confirm identification.

2. Locate it with lines of bearing (LOBs)

  • Click Drop LOB (or press L) to record the current bearing from your position.
  • Let the aircraft move so the bearing changes before dropping the next one. Aim for 30° or more of spread across your LOBs. Nearly parallel lines give a long, skinny AOP.
  • Use Sim speed (bottom right) to speed up the flight while you wait.
  • Three things shrink the AOP: more LOBs, wider spread, and shorter range. Turn the autopilot off and fly toward the emitter if the AOP stays too big.
  • The emitter panel shows an estimated AOP size as you go. With 3 or more LOBs, click Build AOP. Both axes must be under 5 NM. If it's too big, add LOBs (or delete a stray one) and rebuild.

3. Correlate to a platform

Check which platforms carry the radar you identified (shown in the emitter panel and in the Reference library). Emitters on the same bearing may be on the same ship, and their radars together narrow it down. Choose the platform class for the AOP.

4. Export

Click Finish & export, review the summary, and download your results file. Hand that .json file in. Your work is saved in this browser, so if you accidentally close the tab you can resume.

Glossary

ESM
Electronic Support Measures: passively intercepting and analysing emissions.
PRI
Pulse Repetition Interval: time from the start of one pulse to the start of the next (µs).
PW
Pulse Width: how long each pulse lasts (µs).
LOB
Line of Bearing: the direction a signal arrived from, recorded at one moment.
AOP
Area of Probability: the ellipse where several LOBs cross; the emitter is probably inside it.
SMA / SMI
Semi-major / semi-minor axis: half the long and short widths of the AOP ellipse (NM).
